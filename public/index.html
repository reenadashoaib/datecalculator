The formulas, with examples

A percentage is a number out of 100. 25% means 25 out of every 100, which is the same as 0.25 or one quarter. Every calculator above uses one of these formulas.

Percent of a number
P ÷ 100 × N
20% of 150 is 0.2 × 150 = 30.
What percent X is of Y
X ÷ Y × 100
18 out of 24 is 18 ÷ 24 × 100 = 75%.
Percentage change
(new − old) ÷ |old| × 100
A price going from $80 to $100 is (100 − 80) ÷ 80 × 100 = a 25% increase.
Increase or decrease by a percent
N × (1 ± P ÷ 100)
$60 with 15% off is 60 × 0.85 = $51.
Whole from a part
X ÷ (P ÷ 100)
If 12 people are 8% of a group, the group is 12 ÷ 0.08 = 150 people.
Percentage difference
|A − B| ÷ ((A + B) ÷ 2) × 100
Between 40 and 60 the difference is 20 ÷ 50 × 100 = 40%.

Percentage change or percentage difference?

Use percentage change when one value came first, like last month's sales against this month's. The starting value is the base, so going from 50 to 100 is a 100% increase, and going back from 100 to 50 is a 50% decrease.

Use percentage difference when neither value is the starting point, like comparing the prices of two shops. It divides by the average of both, so the answer is the same whichever way round you enter them.

Percentage points

When a rate moves from 4% to 6%, it went up by 2 percentage points, but by 50% in relative terms. News reports and loan offers often mix the two, so check which one is meant.

Common questions

How do I work out a percentage on a phone calculator?

Divide the part by the whole, then multiply by 100. For 18 out of 24, type 18 ÷ 24 × 100 to get 75.

How do I add a percentage to a price, like sales tax?

Multiply the price by 1 plus the rate. For 8% tax on $45, that's 45 × 1.08 = $48.60. Use the increase calculator above to do it for you.

Why doesn't a 10% rise followed by a 10% fall get me back to the start?

The fall is taken from the bigger number. 100 rises 10% to 110, then falls 10% of 110, which is 11, ending at 99.

Can a percentage be more than 100%?

Yes. 150 is 150% of 100, and a value that triples has grown by 200%. Only shares of a single whole, like a pie chart, stop at 100%.

How do I remove the tax from a price that already includes it?

Divide by 1 plus the rate. A $54 bill that includes 8% tax was $54 ÷ 1.08 = $50 before tax.
